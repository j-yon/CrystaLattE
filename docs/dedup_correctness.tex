\begin{abstract}
This note proves that CrystaLatt\'e's multimer deduplication, a single exact
integer \emph{orbit key} (\texttt{invariants.orbit\_labels}), is a sound and
complete decision procedure for the equivalence it targets. Two candidate
$N$-mers are the same multimer when one rigid motion, proper or improper, carries
one onto the other up to an internal symmetry of each monomer
(Definition~\ref{def:same}); merging such clusters leaves the lattice energy
unchanged (Proposition~\ref{prop:energy}). The internal symmetries are drawn from
a per--reference \emph{dressing group} $\Dgrp_i$: the site symmetry together with
every exact self--isometry of the monomer that is also a symmetry of the working
cell (\S\ref{sec:dress}). The global motion cancels from relative transforms,
reducing the question to a finite condition (Theorem~\ref{thm:match}), and the
orbit key is a canonical form for that condition: two clusters share a key
\emph{iff} they are equivalent (Theorem~\ref{thm:key}). The decided relation is
characterised geometrically in Proposition~\ref{prop:reach}: exactly the
equivalences whose global motion maps the cell's lattice box onto itself.
Section~\ref{sec:example} runs the key on real urea and triazine clusters, and
Section~\ref{sec:caveats} lists where the shipped pipeline falls short of the
theorems, including one measured over--merge (ice).
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}

Let $\Eth=\Oth\ltimes\mathbb{R}^3$, an element $T=(R,t)$ acting by $T\cdot x=Rx+t$,
with $(R_1,t_1)(R_2,t_2)=(R_1R_2,\,R_1t_2+t_1)$ and $(R,t)\inv=(R\inv,-R\inv t)$.
A \emph{space group} is a discrete $\Grp\subset\Eth$.

\paragraph{The working basis.} Everything below is written in the lattice basis
of the working cell, with cell matrix $A$ (cell vectors as columns) and metric
$G=A^{\mathsf T}A$. A space--group operation is stored as
$(\mathrm{rot},\mathrm{tr})$ with $\mathrm{rot}\in\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb{Z})$
unimodular and $\mathrm{tr}\in\mathbb{Z}^3$ in \emph{twelfths} of a lattice
vector, so that the operation is $x\mapsto\mathrm{rot}\,x+\mathrm{tr}/12$ on
fractional coordinates. A linear map $h$ in this basis is an isometry iff
$h^{\mathsf T}Gh=G$; it is then orthogonal in Cartesian coordinates as
$AhA\inv$. All arithmetic in the key is on these integers.

\paragraph{References.}
After symmetry detection the asymmetric unit supplies reference monomers
$\mathcal{M}_0,\dots,\mathcal{M}_{r-1}$ ($r=Z'$). Reference $i$ is a labelled
point set $x^{(i)}_1,\dots,x^{(i)}_{m_i}$ (fractional) with atomic numbers
$Z^{(i)}_k$ and mass--weighted centroid $c_i$. Coordinates are \textbf{not}
re--centred, so every statement carries $c_i$ explicitly.

\paragraph{Clusters.}
A candidate $N$-mer $G$ is a tuple of placements
$T^G_0,\dots,T^G_{n-1}\in\Grp$ ($n=N$) with reference labels
$\mathrm{ref}_G(a)$; monomer $a$ is $T^G_a\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{ref}_G(a)}$.
Generation (\texttt{neighbors.generate\_neighbors}) anchors each reference at the
identity, draws partners from one representative per coset of the site symmetry
times lattice translations, and keeps every set of $N-1$ partners whose members
lie pairwise within the cutoff $R$. Each physical multimer is therefore generated
many times: once per choice of anchor, and once per symmetry--related placement.
Deduplication must collapse exactly these repeats.

\section{Dressing groups}\label{sec:dress}

\paragraph{Dressings are affine.}
A self--isometry of a monomer fixes its centroid, so a linear part $s$ acts on
\emph{raw} coordinates as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dressing}
  \sigma_s:x\mapsto s\,x+d_s,\qquad d_s=c_i-s\,c_i .
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[A dressing is determined by its linear part]\label{lem:dress}
If $S$ is a group of linear maps, $\{(s,d_s):s\in S\}$ is a group under the
affine law and $s\mapsto(s,d_s)$ is an isomorphism.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$(s_1,d_{s_1})(s_2,d_{s_2})=(s_1s_2,\ s_1c_i-s_1s_2c_i+c_i-s_1c_i)=(s_1s_2,\ d_{s_1s_2})$,
and $(s,d_s)\inv=(s\inv,-s\inv d_s)=(s\inv,d_{s\inv})$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Requirements.} The key can use any set $\Dgrp_i$ of dressings for
reference $i$ that is
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,label=(R\arabic*)]
\item\label{req:int} \emph{integral}: each linear part is in
  $\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb{Z})$ in the working basis;
\item\label{req:tw} \emph{in twelfths}: each $12\,d_s\in\mathbb{Z}^3$;
\item\label{req:grp} \emph{a group} under composition;
\item\label{req:self} \emph{genuine}: each element maps $\mathcal{M}_i$ onto
  itself, preserving atomic numbers.
\end{enumerate}
\ref{req:int}--\ref{req:tw} make the key exact, \ref{req:grp} makes it a class
invariant (Theorem~\ref{thm:key}), and \ref{req:self} makes merging
energy--preserving (Proposition~\ref{prop:energy}). Nothing requires a dressing
to belong to $\Grp$.

\paragraph{Site symmetry $W_i$.} The stabiliser of $c_i$ in $\Grp$ modulo the
lattice, found at the fractional tolerance \texttt{site\_atol}
(\texttt{dressing.site\_symmetry}). A site operation fixes $c_i$ only modulo a
lattice vector, so that lattice part is removed from its translation, leaving
$\widetilde t_w=12(c_i-w\,c_i)$ exactly as in \eqref{eq:dressing}. $W_i$
satisfies \ref{req:int}--\ref{req:grp} by construction and \ref{req:self} in
exact geometry.

\paragraph{Cell symmetry $\Hgrp$.} The integral isometries of the working
basis, $\Hgrp=\{h\in\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb{Z}):h^{\mathsf T}Gh=G\}$, a finite
group of order at most $48$ (\texttt{dressing.cell\_symmetry}). It is computed in
a Niggli--reduced basis, where every such $h$ has entries in $\{-1,0,1\}$, as the
closure of two sets: the candidates preserving a generic metric averaged over
$\Grp$'s linear parts (the crystal family's cell symmetry, in whatever setting
the CIF uses), and those preserving the actual $G$ (cells more symmetric than
their space group). $\Hgrp$ contains every linear part of $\Grp$. It is the
symmetry of the \emph{box} the cell vectors span, not of the Bravais lattice: an
R--centred cell on hexagonal axes has $|\Hgrp|=24$ ($6/mmm$), not the R
lattice's $12$.

\paragraph{The dressing group.}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D}
  \Dgrp_i=W_i\ \cup\ \bigl\{h\in\Hgrp:\ \sigma_h\text{ maps }\mathcal{M}_i\text{ onto itself to }10^{-6}\text{\,\AA},\
  12(c_i-h\,c_i)\in\mathbb{Z}^3\bigr\},
\end{equation}
built by \texttt{dressing.dressing\_group}. If the union is not closed under
composition it falls back to $W_i$ with a warning; exact operations whose
translation is not in twelfths are skipped with a warning.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:D}
$W_i\le\Dgrp_i\le\Hgrp$, and $\Dgrp_i$ satisfies
\ref{req:int}--\ref{req:self} (\ref{req:self} up to the stated tolerances).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$W_i$'s linear parts lie in $\Grp$'s, hence in $\Hgrp$; added elements are drawn
from $\Hgrp$, which gives \ref{req:int}. \ref{req:tw} is the admission test.
\ref{req:grp} is checked, with $W_i$ as fallback. \ref{req:self} holds for $W_i$
by crystal symmetry and is tested directly for every added element.
\end{proof}

On the X23 and auxiliary fixtures $\Dgrp_i=W_i$ except for triazine
($6\to12$) and carbon dioxide ($6\to12$).

\section{The equivalence}

\begin{definition}[Same multimer]\label{def:same}
$G\sim H$ iff there are $M\in\Eth$, a permutation $\pi$ with
$\mathrm{ref}_H(\pi(a))=\mathrm{ref}_G(a)$, and
$\sigma_a\in\Dgrp_{\mathrm{ref}_G(a)}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:star}
  M\,T^G_a=T^H_{\pi(a)}\,\sigma_a\qquad(0\le a<n). \tag{$\star$}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

One rigid motion $M$ carries the whole of $G$ onto the whole of $H$; $\pi$
discards the order in which members were listed; and $\sigma_a$ absorbs the fact
that two placements of a symmetric molecule can differ as elements of $\Eth$
while putting every atom in the same place. $M$ ranges over all of $\Eth$, so
mirror images are included: for an interaction energy an enantiomeric pair of
clusters is one multimer.

\begin{proposition}[Merging is safe for the lattice sum]\label{prop:energy}
If $G\sim H$, the two clusters have congruent $Z$--labelled nuclear clouds and
hence equal interaction energy.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \ref{req:self}, $\sigma_a X_i=X_i$ as a labelled set, where $X_i$ is reference
$i$'s atoms. So
$M\,T^G_aX_{\mathrm{ref}_G(a)}=T^H_{\pi(a)}\sigma_aX_{\mathrm{ref}_G(a)}
=T^H_{\pi(a)}X_{\mathrm{ref}_H(\pi(a))}$, which is monomer $\pi(a)$ of $H$. As
$\pi$ is a bijection, $M$ carries $G$'s atoms onto $H$'s with atomic numbers
preserved, and the energy is invariant under isometries.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[$\sim$ is an equivalence relation]\label{prop:equiv}
If every $\Dgrp_i$ is a group, $\sim$ is reflexive, symmetric and transitive.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Reflexive: $M=\id$, $\pi=\id$, $\sigma_a=\id$. Symmetric: $M\inv
T^H_{\pi(a)}=T^G_a\sigma_a\inv$, reindexed by $b=\pi(a)$. Transitive: composing
$M_1T^G_a=T^H_{\pi_1(a)}\sigma_a$ with $M_2T^H_b=T^K_{\pi_2(b)}\tau_b$ gives
$M_2M_1T^G_a=T^K_{\pi_2\pi_1(a)}\tau_{\pi_1(a)}\sigma_a$, and the product
dressing lies in $\Dgrp_{\mathrm{ref}_G(a)}$ by closure, since
$\mathrm{ref}_H(\pi_1(a))=\mathrm{ref}_G(a)$.
\end{proof}

\section{The matching condition}

\paragraph{Relative transforms.}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel}
  \Delta^G_{ab}=(T^G_a)\inv T^G_b=(L^G_{ab},v^G_{ab}),\quad
  L^G_{ab}=R_a\inv R_b,\quad v^G_{ab}=R_a\inv(t_b-t_a).
\end{equation}
In the working basis $L$ is integral and $v$ is in integral twelfths. Two
identities matter: the cocycle law $\Delta_{ab}\Delta_{bc}=\Delta_{ac}$, and
invariance under $G\mapsto MG$ for every $M\in\Eth$, since $M$ cancels in
$(MT_a)\inv(MT_b)$.

\begin{theorem}[Matching condition]\label{thm:match}
$G\sim H$ iff there are a reference--compatible $\pi$ and
$\sigma_a\in\Dgrp_{\mathrm{ref}_G(a)}$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dagger}
  \Delta^G_{ab}=\sigma_a\inv\,\Delta^H_{\pi(a)\pi(b)}\,\sigma_b
  \qquad\text{for all }a,b. \tag{$\dagger$}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
($\Rightarrow$) From \eqref{eq:star},
$\Delta^G_{ab}=\sigma_a\inv(T^H_{\pi(a)})\inv MM\inv T^H_{\pi(b)}\sigma_b
=\sigma_a\inv\Delta^H_{\pi(a)\pi(b)}\sigma_b$.
($\Leftarrow$) Put $M:=T^H_{\pi(0)}\sigma_0(T^G_0)\inv$. The $(0,a)$ instance of
\eqref{eq:dagger} rearranges to
$T^H_{\pi(a)}\sigma_a(T^G_a)\inv=T^H_{\pi(0)}\sigma_0(T^G_0)\inv=M$, which is
\eqref{eq:star}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[One anchor row suffices]\label{lem:row}
Fix $\pi$, the $\sigma$'s and any anchor $a_0$. If \eqref{eq:dagger} holds for
every pair $(a_0,b)$ it holds for every pair $(a,b)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\Delta^G_{ab}=(\Delta^G_{a_0a})\inv\Delta^G_{a_0b}
=\sigma_a\inv\Delta^H_{\pi(a)\pi(a_0)}\sigma_{a_0}\sigma_{a_0}\inv
\Delta^H_{\pi(a_0)\pi(b)}\sigma_b=\sigma_a\inv\Delta^H_{\pi(a)\pi(b)}\sigma_b$.
\end{proof}

The global motion, a six--parameter continuum, has dropped out entirely; it is
never formed or searched for. Because it cancels whatever its determinant, the
procedure is blind to $\det M$, and mirror--related clusters need no special case.
What remains is a finite condition on integers.

\paragraph{What the dressing group buys.}
The integrality of $\Dgrp$ fixes precisely which equivalences can be decided.

\begin{proposition}[Reach of the key]\label{prop:reach}
Suppose $\Dgrp_i$ contains every self--isometry of $\mathcal{M}_i$ whose linear
part lies in $\Hgrp$ and whose translation is in twelfths. Then $G\sim H$ iff some
$(M,\pi,\sigma)$, with $\sigma_a$ any self--isometries whose translations are
in twelfths, satisfies \eqref{eq:star} with the linear part of $M$ in $\Hgrp$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
From \eqref{eq:star}, $\mathrm{lin}(\sigma_a)=R^H_{\pi(a)}{}\inv\,\mathrm{lin}(M)\,R^G_a$.
The placements' linear parts lie in $\Grp$'s, hence in $\Hgrp$, so
$\mathrm{lin}(\sigma_a)\in\Hgrp\iff\mathrm{lin}(M)\in\Hgrp$.
\end{proof}

In words: the key merges two clusters exactly when a motion relating them maps
the lattice box onto itself. That includes every space--group operation and
lattice translation, and also exact molecular symmetry that is not
crystallographic but is integral in the working cell (triazine's $\sigma_h$,
\S\ref{sec:example}). It excludes molecular symmetry whose orientation is set by
free atomic coordinates rather than the lattice (Caveat~\ref{cav:reach}).

\section{The orbit key decides the matching condition}\label{sec:key}

\paragraph{Cosets.} Seen from an anchor $a$, partner $b$ is the relative
transform $\Delta_{ab}$, but its own dressing relabels it without moving an atom:
the partner is really the set $\Delta_{ab}\Dgrp_b=\{\Delta_{ab}w:w\in\Dgrp_b\}$.
Relabelling the anchor by $s\in\Dgrp_a$ turns this into
$s\inv\Delta_{ab}\Dgrp_b$. Writing elements as integer $12$--vectors
$(\mathrm{vec}\,L,v)$, the affine law gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row}
  s\inv\Delta w=\bigl(s\inv L\,w,\ s\inv(L\,d_w+v-d_s)\bigr),
\end{equation}
all integer (unimodular $s\inv$, twelfths $d$). Let
$\rho(a,s,b)=\lexmin\,s\inv\Delta_{ab}\Dgrp_b$, the coset's lexicographically
smallest element (\texttt{\_dressed\_minima}).

\begin{lemma}[Coset minima are canonical]\label{lem:coset}
Since $\Dgrp_b$ is a group, $x\Dgrp_b$ and $y\Dgrp_b$ are equal or disjoint, so
$\lexmin x\Dgrp_b=\lexmin y\Dgrp_b\iff x\Dgrp_b=y\Dgrp_b\iff x\inv y\in\Dgrp_b$.
\end{lemma}

\paragraph{The key.} Order the reference labels
$r_0\le r_1\le\dots$ of $G$. For each anchor $a$ with
$\mathrm{ref}_G(a)=r_0$ and each $s\in\Dgrp_{r_0}$, form the vector
$K(a,s)$ of the partner rows $\rho(a,s,b)$, $b\ne a$, grouped by partner reference
in increasing order and sorted within each group (partners of one reference are
interchangeable). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:key}
  \key(G)=\bigl(\,(r_0,r_1,\dots),\ \min_{a,\,s}K(a,s)\bigr).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[The key is complete and sound]\label{thm:key}
$\key(G)=\key(H)\iff G\sim H$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
($\Leftarrow$) Take $(\pi,\sigma)$ from Theorem~\ref{thm:match}. For an anchor $a$
and $s\in\Dgrp_a$, \eqref{eq:dagger} and $\sigma_b\Dgrp_b=\Dgrp_b$ give
\[
  s\inv\Delta^G_{ab}\Dgrp_b=(\sigma_as)\inv\Delta^H_{\pi(a)\pi(b)}\sigma_b\Dgrp_b
  =(\sigma_as)\inv\Delta^H_{\pi(a)\pi(b)}\Dgrp_b ,
\]
so $\rho_G(a,s,b)=\rho_H(\pi(a),\sigma_as,\pi(b))$. Because $\pi$ is a
reference--compatible bijection, the grouped, sorted rows agree:
$K_G(a,s)=K_H(\pi(a),\sigma_as)$. The map $(a,s)\mapsto(\pi(a),\sigma_as)$ is a
bijection of anchor--dressing pairs, so the minima agree; the reference tuples
agree because $\pi$ is reference--compatible.

($\Rightarrow$) Equal keys give equal reference tuples and pairs $(a,s)$,
$(a',s')$ with $K_G(a,s)=K_H(a',s')$. Set $\pi(a)=a'$ and pair the remaining
members by equal rows within each reference group. For each matched $b\mapsto b'$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:coset} gives $w_b\in\Dgrp_b$ with
$s\inv\Delta^G_{ab}=s'^{-1}\Delta^H_{a'b'}w_b$, i.e.\
$\Delta^G_{ab}=\sigma_a\inv\Delta^H_{\pi(a)\pi(b)}\sigma_b$ with
$\sigma_a=s's\inv\in\Dgrp_{r_0}$ and $\sigma_b=w_b$. That is \eqref{eq:dagger}
on the anchor row of $a$, hence everywhere by Lemma~\ref{lem:row}, and $G\sim H$
by Theorem~\ref{thm:match}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Exactness]
Every quantity in \eqref{eq:key} is an integer, and equality is
\texttt{np.array\_equal}. No tolerance enters per cluster, so the decision does
not degrade with cluster size or separation, and $\sim$ as computed is exactly
transitive. Tolerances enter only once per reference, in building $W_i$ and
$\Dgrp_i$ (\S\ref{sec:caveats}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The affine part is load--bearing]\label{rem:centering}
Dropping $d$ is valid only when every dressing fixes the origin, i.e.\
$s\,c_i=c_i$. That holds for a molecule centred at a fixed point of its site
symmetry (benzene) and fails otherwise (urea). Since $s$ preserves norms, the
failure cannot be repaired by any tolerance (\S\ref{sec:example}).
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Cost and representatives.} The key is computed for all candidates at
once, grouped by reference tuple. Each \emph{distinct} relative transform is
dressed once, at cost $|\Dgrp_a||\Dgrp_b|$, rows are packed into order--preserving
\texttt{int64} codes (\texttt{lex\_codes}), and classes are found by sorting, so
no pair of candidates is ever compared directly. Each class keeps one
representative (smallest summed placement translation) carrying the summed
multiplicity of the class (\texttt{equivalence.merge}). By
Proposition~\ref{prop:energy} the multiplicity--weighted lattice sum is unchanged.

\section{Worked examples}\label{sec:example}

\paragraph{Urea: the affine dressing.} \texttt{tests/x23/urea/urea.cif},
$P\bar42_1m$ (No.~113), $Z'=1$. The centroid is $c=(0,-\tfrac12,0.316143)$, and
$\Dgrp=W$ has order $4$:
\[
\begin{array}{llll}
w_0=I, & \widetilde t_0=(0,0,0);
&w_1=\begin{psmallmatrix}-1&0&0\\0&-1&0\\0&0&1\end{psmallmatrix},
&\widetilde t_1=(0,-12,0);\\[2.2ex]
w_2=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&1&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&1\end{psmallmatrix},
&\widetilde t_2=(6,-6,0);
&w_3=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&-1&0\\-1&0&0\\0&0&1\end{psmallmatrix},
&\widetilde t_3=(-6,-6,0).
\end{array}
\]
$w_1$ is the molecule's two--fold axis, which passes through $y=-\tfrac12$, not
the origin, so $\widetilde t_1\ne0$. With
$P=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&1&0\\-1&0&0\\0&0&-1\end{psmallmatrix}$ (the $\bar4$ of
$\Grp$), take the dimers
\[
  G:\ (I,\mathbf 0),\ (P,\mathbf 0);\qquad
  H:\ (I,\mathbf 0),\ (P,(0,-12,0)),
\]
the same $\bar4$ image, translated by $-b$ in $H$. Then
$\Delta^G_{01}=(P,\mathbf 0)$ and $\Delta^H_{10}=(P\inv,(-12,0,0))$. Anchoring $G$
at member $0$ and $H$ at member $1$, both with $s=I$, \eqref{eq:row} gives
\[
  \Delta^H_{10}\,\sigma_{w_1}=\bigl(P\inv w_1,\ P\inv(0,-12,0)+(-12,0,0)\bigr)
  =\bigl(P,\ (12,0,0)+(-12,0,0)\bigr)=\Delta^G_{01},
\]
since $P^2=w_1$. The two partner cosets coincide, the rows agree, and the keys are
equal: the two dimers are one dimer viewed from opposite ends, related by
composing the $\bar4$ placement with the molecule's own two--fold axis. Zero the
dressing translations and the same comparison asks whether
$(0,0,0)=(-12,0,0)$, which fails by a full lattice repeat ($5.59$\,\AA), and no
other dressing can help, because each preserves $\lVert v\rVert$. Both outcomes
are what the code produces (\texttt{test\_orbit\_label\_needs\_dressing\_translation}).

\paragraph{Triazine: symmetry beyond the space group.} $R\bar3c$ (No.~167) on
hexagonal axes, $Z'=1$. The site symmetry has order $6$. The hexagonal box has
$|\Hgrp|=24$, of which only $12$ preserve the R lattice. The molecule's
$D_{3h}$ supplies six further operations, all improper ($\sigma_h$, two $S_3$,
three $\sigma_v$), all integral in the hexagonal basis and exact to
$10^{-15}$\,\AA, all outside the R lattice's $\bar3m$. So $|\Dgrp|=12$. They
reproduce exactly the merges a Cartesian point--group search used to find
($61$ unique dimers to $20$\,\AA, $266$ to $35$\,\AA, $123$ trimers to
$12$\,\AA), now in integer arithmetic. Computing $\Hgrp$ from the Bravais
lattice instead of the box would lose them
(\texttt{test\_cell\_symmetry\_is\_the\_box\_not\_the\_bravais\_lattice}).

\section{Caveats}\label{sec:caveats}

Theorem~\ref{thm:key} is exact given the dressing groups. The following are the
ways the deployed pipeline is weaker than it.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,label=\textbf{C\arabic*.}]

\item\label{cav:reach}\textbf{Completeness is relative to $\Hgrp$.} By
Proposition~\ref{prop:reach}, equivalences whose relating motion is not integral
in the working cell are not merged. Such a motion can relate two clusters only if
a molecular symmetry axis, oriented by free atomic coordinates, happens to carry
lattice vectors onto lattice vectors. For rigid molecules this is a coincidence,
not something the structure forces. Ammonia's $\sigma_v$ is exact but outside
$\Hgrp$, and produced no merges in any measured run when a Cartesian search was
still available. Missed merges cost redundant calculations, never accuracy: the
split representatives are congruent and their multiplicities sum correctly.

\item\label{cav:W}\textbf{The site symmetry is trusted, not verified, and this
over--merges ice.} $W_i$ enters $\Dgrp_i$ unconditionally, on the strength of the
site test at \texttt{site\_atol}. For ordered crystals this is exact: the atoms
were generated by those operations (worst same--element residual $\le10^{-15}$\,\AA{}
for triazine, ammonia, benzene and carbon dioxide). For ice, whose proton positions
are a disorder model resolved to one configuration, the site operations fit the
monomer only to $3.8\times10^{-4}$\,\AA. They are not genuine self--isometries, so
\ref{req:self} fails, and the key merges clusters that are not congruent: $R=15$
dimers drop from $208$ to $78$ from the same $497$ candidates, and the over--merge
referee confirms real energy differences. This is the unrecoverable direction of
error, since a false merge transfers multiplicity between clusters of different
energy and nothing cancels it. Ice is pinned as a strict \texttt{xfail}; how to
resolve it (accept, test $W$ against the atoms, or symmetrise the monomer) is
open.

\item\label{cav:tol}\textbf{Added dressings are admitted at a tolerance.} An
operation of $\Hgrp\setminus W_i$ joins $\Dgrp_i$ only if it fits the monomer to
$10^{-6}$\,\AA. On every fixture, accepted operations fit to $\le10^{-15}$\,\AA{}
and no rejected one comes within $10^{-3}$\,\AA, so the threshold is far from any
decision. Approximate molecular symmetry (benzene's $D_{6h}$ in its crystal,
worst residual $\sim10^{-2}$\,\AA) is correctly refused: it could not relate
clusters exactly at any separation, since a rotation error $\delta$ displaces a
partner by $\delta\lVert v\rVert$.

\item\label{cav:fallback}\textbf{Guarded fallbacks.} If $W_i$ and the exact
extra operations do not close into a group, $\Dgrp_i$ falls back to $W_i$; if the
two candidate sets for $\Hgrp$ do not close within order $48$ (an inconsistent
cell), $\Hgrp$ falls back to the space--group lookup; an exact operation whose
translation is not in twelfths is skipped. Each warns, and each errs towards
fewer dressings, i.e.\ towards keeping duplicates. No fixture triggers any of
them. The completeness of the $\{-1,0,1\}$ candidates rests on running the search
in a Niggli--reduced basis.

\item\label{cav:linear}\textbf{Linear monomers are incomplete by construction.}
A placement fixes three rotational degrees of freedom, a linear molecule's
orientation only two; the twist about the molecular axis moves no atom but is
still compared in $L_{ab}$. Quotienting a continuous twist needs a continuous
dressing group, and every $\Dgrp_i$ is finite. Integral twists help: carbon
dioxide ($Pa\bar3$, $|W|=6$, $|\Dgrp|=12$) now gives the correct $36$ dimers at
$R=15$ and $81$ (from $86$) at $R=20$, every merge refereed, but one congruent
pair of representatives remains at $R=20$. \texttt{generate} warns for linear
monomers.

\item\label{cav:zprime}\textbf{Cross--reference congruence is left to symmetry
detection.} $\pi$ must be reference--compatible, so two independent references
are never identified with each other. Two independent molecules identical to
within tolerance normally mean the space group has missed symmetry, which
\texttt{detect\_symmetry} restores before dedup. With detection off, such
duplicates survive (with a warning). Genuine $Z'>1$ crystals are handled directly:
each member is dressed by its own reference's group, and pyrazole ($Z'=2$) and
fomepizole ($Z'=3$) reproduce every merge of the former point--cloud matcher.

\item\label{cav:gen}\textbf{Generation--side assumptions.} The theorems presume
the candidate set is right. Partners are drawn from one representative per coset
of $W_i$ at \texttt{site\_atol}, which must be the same tolerance used to build
$W_i$ here, or orbit and stabiliser disagree; it cannot be made arbitrarily
tight, because CIFs write special positions such as $(1/3,2/3,z)$ as rounded
decimals.

\end{enumerate}
